Evaluation of Voice Agents

A full-day ICASSP 2027 workshop bringing the speech and NLP communities together to build shared methodologies, benchmarks, and metrics for evaluating speech-based agentic systems.

About EVA

Speech-based agentic systems, such as voice assistants, spoken dialogue agents, and speech-to-speech models, are advancing rapidly, but their evaluation has not kept pace. Speech agents must be assessed not only on what they say (content, task success, factuality) but on how they say it (prosody, timing, turn-taking, backchannels). The speech processing and NLP communities pursue these two dimensions largely in isolation, and evaluation increasingly relies on simulated users whose realism is rarely measured.

EVA brings these communities together to build shared methodologies, benchmarks, and metrics for evaluating speech-based agentic systems, independent of architecture, and welcomes researchers working on text-based dialogue evaluation as well.

Call for Papers

  • Speaker behavior: benchmarking turn-taking, timing, backchannels, and full-duplex behavior; speech naturalness evaluation; conversation analysis applied to human-agent interaction.
  • Content: dialogue- and turn-level quality, task success, partial goal achievement, factual grounding, and tool use in multi-step tasks.
  • Methodology: LLM/LALM-as-judge protocols and their calibration and agreement with human raters; user simulation and simulator realism measures; fair comparison across architectures.
  • Text-based dialogue evaluation: we explicitly welcome content-dimension contributions from the text-based dialogue evaluation community.

Archival: published in IEEE Xplore. Non-archival: presented at the workshop.

Organizers

Mahnoosh Mehrabani

SoundHound AI

Director of ML Engineering at SoundHound AI, where she leads a team building an end-to-end evaluation and robustness testing framework for customer-facing speech-based agentic systems. She received her doctorate from UT Dallas under Prof. John H. L. Hansen in 2013 and co-led a JSALT 2026 project on evaluating the reliability of agentic systems for health coaching.

Ondrej Klejch

University of Edinburgh

Senior researcher in the Centre for Speech Technology Research, School of Informatics, University of Edinburgh. His work focuses on building automatic speech recognition systems with limited training data and supervision. He recently led the JSALT 2026 project on simulating and evaluating spoken conversations.

Jan Niehues

Karlsruhe Institute of Technology

Professor at KIT, where he leads the AI for Language Technologies group. His research focuses on multimodal foundation models, multilingual large language models, and human-AI communication. He is active in the spoken language translation community and currently serves as an organizer of IWSLT.

Maike Züfle

Karlsruhe Institute of Technology

PhD student at KIT Karlsruhe and Apple AI/ML Fellow. Her research focuses on instruction-following speech models with speech as both input and output, with a focus on full-duplex models and speech evaluation. She co-organizes the instruction-following and speech translation metrics shared tasks at IWSLT, as well as the Multilingual Instruction Shared Task at EMNLP.

Srinivas Bangalore

SoundHound AI

VP of Engineering at SoundHound AI. He has published in several areas of speech and language processing, including speech translation, multimodal understanding, question answering, and more recently conversational systems, with over 200 publications and over 150 patents.
